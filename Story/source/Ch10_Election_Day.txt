SUPER| WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 6, 2024 — 3:22 AM PST
ACT| Everyone has gone. The televisions are off. Confetti on the carpet. The velvet couch, its cushions dented, still in front of the biggest screen.
ACT| Marc sits on it alone. Shoes off. Tie gone. The President-elect of the United States, in a dark room under a football stadium, at 3:22 in the morning.
ACT| The door opens. Cornelius, in his socks, holding his shoes. He's been walking the empty concourse for an hour.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| I couldn't sleep.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| Me neither.
ACT| Cornelius sits down on the couch next to him. They sit there in the dark for a while.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| Your mother laughed at something my mother said tonight.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| I heard.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| Sixty years. Smilleys and Donohues. I never thought I'd hear that.
PAR| (beat)
DLG| Joan came up to me after the speech. On the field.
ACT| Marc turns to look at him.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| What did she say?
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| She said, "He would have been so proud of you, Cornelius."
PAR| (beat)
DLG| Fifty-five years. That's the first thing she's said to me since 1969.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| What did you say?
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| I said, "Thank you, Joan."
PAR| (beat)
DLG| And then I said, "He'd have been proud of you too." And I meant it.
ACT| A long silence.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| Dad. Can I ask you for something?
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| You're the President-elect. You can ask for anything.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| I need five more states. Kentucky's calling a session. Missouri's Speaker likes Arianna but won't commit. Montana's legislative counsel's dog hates her. And there are three more where the people who count the votes in the statehouse go to church on Sunday with pastors you've known for forty years.
ACT| Cornelius turns to look at him.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| I need a firewall. The best one anybody ever built.
ACT| A long, long beat. Then, slowly, Cornelius Smilley smiles. A real one. Wide. The smile that won him eight elections.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| Son. I thought you'd never ask.
ACT| He reaches into his breast pocket. Not the twins' card this time. A folded sheet of yellow legal-pad paper. He hands it to Marc.
ACT| INSERT — THE LEGAL PAD: "28TH — THE FIVE. 1. KENTUCKY — Rev. Ellison, Frankfort. Owes me for 1998. 2. MISSOURI — Speaker's mother-in-law, St. Louis AME. 3. MONTANA — leave to Arianna. (The dog.) 4. — 5. —"
ACT| Marc reads it. Looks up.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| When did you write this?
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| On the plane. Yesterday morning. Before a single vote was counted.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| You didn't know if I'd win.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| I'm a Smilley, son. We count votes.
PAR| (beat)
DLG| I knew.
ACT| He stands. Picks up his shoes. Walks to the door. Stops.
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| And Marc.
CHAR| MARC
DLG| Yeah?
CHAR| CORNELIUS
DLG| Congratulations, Mr. President.
ACT| The door closes.
ACT| Marc sits alone on the couch. Looks down at the legal pad. At numbers four and five, still blank.
ACT| On his phone, on the cushion beside him, a news alert lights up the dark room:
ACT| INSERT — PHONE SCREEN: "28TH AMENDMENT TRACKER — 33 OF 38 STATES — 75 DAYS UNTIL INAUGURATION."
ACT| He looks at it for a long time. Then he smiles. Stands up. Slips his shoes back on.
ACT| And, alone, in the dark, the President-elect of the United States steps up onto the couch one more time. Raises his fist.
CHAR| MARC
PAR| (quietly, to no one)
DLG| ...Bet.
