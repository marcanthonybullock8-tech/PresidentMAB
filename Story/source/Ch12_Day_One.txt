SLUG| MONTAGE — JANUARY 5 TO JANUARY 20, 2025
ACT| — SUNDAY, JANUARY 5, 2025. Sunrise Hill. The President-elect turns thirty-three. His birthday cake, designed by Victoria, is shaped like the Resolute Desk. Emma has added fondant footnotes.
ACT| — MONDAY, JANUARY 6, 1:00 PM EST. The House chamber. Four years to the day after the attack on the Capitol, a joint session of Congress meets to count the electoral votes. Presiding, as President of the Senate: VICE PRESIDENT KAMALA HARRIS (60). She reads the certificates state by state. Georgia: sixteen votes, signed in flowing cursive by an elector named Odessa Grant Smilley. When she announces the total, "Marc-Anthony Bullock of the State of California has received three hundred fifty-nine votes," the chamber rises. Democrats and Republicans alike.
ACT| — TUESDAY, JANUARY 7. Los Angeles. Hurricane-force Santa Ana winds. Fire erupts in the Pacific Palisades, and that evening in Altadena, the foothill town that was one of the first places in Southern California where Black families could buy homes. By morning, entire neighborhoods are gone.
ACT| — WEDNESDAY, JANUARY 8, 6:14 AM PST. Altadena. Smoke. Ash falling like snow. The President-elect, in a respirator and a fire department jacket, standing in the ruins of a block on Lake Avenue with a Black grandmother who has just learned her house of fifty-one years is gone. No cameras. Dante made sure. She holds his hand and tells him her husband built the back porch himself in 1974. Marc writes it down in a notebook.
ACT| — THURSDAY, JANUARY 9. The Washington National Cathedral. The state funeral of the thirty-ninth President, Jimmy Carter, who died December twenty-ninth at a hundred years old. Five Presidents, past and future, in the front pews. The President-elect sits beside Esther, three rows behind Joe Biden and Donald Trump.
ACT| — SUNDAY, JANUARY 19. In Gaza, the first phase of a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas takes effect after fifteen months of war. The first three hostages come home. The President-elect issues a one-line statement: "Every hostage home. Every civilian protected. We start tomorrow."
ACT| — Weather forecasters warn that Inauguration Day in Washington will be the coldest in forty years. Officials move the ceremony indoors, to the Capitol Rotunda, for the first time since 1985.
