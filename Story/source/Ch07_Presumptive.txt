SUB| COLD OPEN — "THE THIRD PEW"

SLUG| INT. GREATER MOUNT CALVARY BAPTIST CHURCH — SANCTUARY — ATLANTA — MORNING
SUPER| SUNDAY, MARCH 10, 2024 — 11:02 AM EDT
SUPER| (DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME BEGAN AT 2:00 AM)
ACT| A red-brick church built in 1923, the same year as Donohue Entertainment, though no one here would appreciate the comparison. Stained-glass windows of Moses, of Ruth, of a Black Jesus with his arms open. Twelve hundred people in their Sunday best. Ushers in white gloves. Fans from a funeral home that closed in 1988.
ACT| Behind the pulpit, in a gold frame, a photograph of the Rev. Dr. Josiah Smilley preaching in 1965, one hand raised.
ACT| In the third pew from the front, on the left side, where she has sat every Sunday since 1952: ODESSA SMILLEY (95), in a white church hat with a brim like a satellite dish. On her right, LORRAINE (62). Beside her, SIMONE (36). On the aisle, NEIL (39), in his "BET." pin, which an usher asked him to remove and he politely declined.
ACT| On Odessa's left: an empty seat.
ACT| The PASTOR, REV. DR. CALVIN HOLLOWAY (70), large, gravel-voiced, Josiah's successor's successor, approaches the pulpit.
CHAR| REV. HOLLOWAY
DLG| Church, we have a guest in the pulpit this morning. Not a preacher. Not a politician. A daughter of this house. She was baptized right there, in that pool, in 1993, by my predecessor, and she screamed so loud the deacons thought the water was boiling.
ACT| Laughter ripples through the sanctuary.
CHAR| REV. HOLLOWAY
DLG| She's been in the movies. She's been on the television. She married a man some of y'all have strong opinions about.
PAR| (louder laughter)
DLG| But this morning she's just Odessa Smilley's granddaughter. Esther, come on up, baby.
ACT| From the front pew, ESTHER BULLOCK (31) rises. A simple navy dress. Her grandmother's pearls. No campaign button. No "BET." She walks up the three steps to the pulpit where her grandfather preached for fifty years.
ACT| She grips the sides of it. Looks out at twelve hundred faces. Many of them knew her when she was a baby.
ACT| And then, from the back of the sanctuary, the double doors open.
ACT| Every head turns.
ACT| CORNELIUS SMILLEY (65), in a charcoal suit and a burgundy tie, the one his father wore on Sundays, walks down the center aisle. Slowly. He doesn't look left or right. Every person in the church knows exactly who he is and exactly what this means.
ACT| He reaches the third pew. Odessa, without looking at him, moves her pocketbook from the empty seat to her lap.
ACT| He sits down next to his mother.
ACT| Esther, in the pulpit, watches him do it. Her hands tighten on the wood. For a second, she can't speak.
ACT| Then she does.
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| Good morning, Greater Mount Calvary.
CHAR| CONGREGATION
DLG| Good morning!
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| I'm not here to tell you who to vote for on Tuesday.
PAR| (a few knowing chuckles)
DLG| I'm serious. My husband would like me to. My campaign manager would like me to. My daddy would like me to tell you the opposite.
PAR| (louder laughter; Cornelius doesn't move)
DLG| But my grandmother taught me in this building that you don't stand in God's house and campaign. You stand in God's house and tell the truth. So here's the truth.
ACT| She looks down at the third pew. At her father.
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| When I was nineteen years old, I did something my father didn't approve of. I fell in love with somebody he didn't choose. I had two baby girls before I had a wedding ring. And for eleven years, I've been afraid to walk into this church, because I thought the people who loved me here would see me the way he did.
ACT| The sanctuary is completely silent.
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| And then my grandmother called me and said, "Baby, come home and sit in your bedroom upstairs, and on Sunday you come to church, and if anybody looks at you sideways, you send them to me."
PAR| (a wave of laughter and "Amen!" — Odessa nods regally)
DLG| So I'm home. And here's what I learned in eleven years. My grandfather, whose picture is right behind me, was a great man. And he was a sinner. And both of those things are true. My father is a great man. And he's wrong about my husband. Both of those things are true too.
ACT| A murmur. Cornelius's jaw tightens. He stares straight ahead.
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| And my husband is a good man. He's also thirty-two years old, and he says things on stage that make church mothers clutch their pearls, and he'd stand on this pulpit if Pastor let him.
PAR| (laughter)
DLG| Both of those things are true.
PAR| (beat)
DLG| So on Tuesday, vote for whoever you think tells you the truth. And whoever you vote for, come home to this church on Sunday and sit next to the people who voted the other way. Because that's what family is. That's what this church is. That's what this country is supposed to be. You can disagree with somebody all the way to the ballot box and still pass them the greens at dinner.
ACT| She looks directly at her father.
CHAR| ESTHER
DLG| I love you, Daddy. I'm not on your side. But I love you.
ACT| A beat. Twelve hundred people hold their breath.
ACT| Cornelius doesn't move. Doesn't nod. Doesn't speak.
ACT| But his hand, resting on his knee, slowly turns over, palm up. A gesture so small that only three people in the church see it. Odessa sees it. Lorraine sees it. And Esther sees it from the pulpit.
ACT| Esther steps back from the microphone. The organist, who has been waiting thirty years to play something this loud, launches into "Blessed Assurance." The congregation rises, singing, and the moment dissolves into music.
ACT| In the third pew, Odessa, singing, reaches over without looking and puts her hand in her son's upturned palm. He closes his fingers around it.
